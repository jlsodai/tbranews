The Road to AFD 2026

100 Days to Addis: The Next Act Begins

The countdown is officially on. In just 100 days, the Africa Private Sector Forum on Displacement will convene in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, from 15–18 November 2026 for its fourth edition.

Since Kigali in 2021, Accra in 2023 and Nairobi in 2025, the Forum has brought together more than 3,000 leaders across business, government, investment, development and displacement-affected communities. Along the way, one thing has become clear: economic inclusion works. The challenge now is taking what works to scale.

Under the theme, “The Next Act: Creating A More Inclusive Economic Ecosystem,” Addis 2026 will focus on scaling proven approaches to jobs, investment, enterprise growth, policy reform and market access for displaced people and host communities.

Join us in Addis Ababa as we move from what works to what can scale. Register your interest today to be part of the Africa Private Sector Forum on Displacement 2026.

Amahoro Coalition

How Ethiopia Inspired Amina’s Pan-African Story

For Lilly Bekele, Amina is more than a fashion brand. It is a tribute to heritage, memory and the generations that came before her. At its heart is Gabi, Ethiopia’s centuries-old hand-spun, handwoven cotton, reimagined alongside textiles and craftsmanship from Burkina Faso, Mali, Côte d’Ivoire and across the continent.

The brand takes its name from Lilly’s great-grandmother, Amina, who was forcibly displaced from Somalia to Ethiopia before settling in Harar. Her story of resilience, belonging and rebuilding continues to shape Lilly’s vision for an Ethiopian-born brand with a Pan-African heart.

That vision has also translated into action. A partnership with Amahoro Coalition has enabled Amina to support the Coalition’s internship programme and connect with displaced tailoring talent, ultimately leading to the hiring of two new team members. In doing so, Amina is putting its values of inclusion, dignity and opportunity into practice as the business grows.

In this conversation with Lilly we talk about the heritage behind Amina, her journey from development professional to entrepreneur, and why building a truly Pan-African business means creating opportunity along the way.

Inkomoko

Reframing Partnerships to Unlock Market Opportunities

Over the past quarter, Inkomoko’s REframe campaign called on the private sector to rethink displacement by recognizing those who have been forcibly displaced as economic actors and responding through investment and partnerships.

Dawit Tilahun, Inkomoko Ethiopia Managing Director, builds on this message in the podcast Reframing Refugee Potential: Entrepreneurship & Digital Skills. He makes the case that economic inclusion cannot be delivered by one organization or intervention alone. Governments must remove policy barriers, financial institutions must develop appropriate financing, and businesses must open markets, supply chains, and distribution channels.

Samuel Layne, a furniture maker and entrepreneur in Addis Ababa, is one of many entrepreneurs whose experience demonstrates the value of this approach. His business already had customers, skills, and demand, but without a work permit, he could not sign contracts or open a bank account. After securing a work permit, Samuel expanded his workshop, opened a furniture store, more than doubled his income, and created seven jobs.

Since 2022, Inkomoko has worked with Ethiopia’s Refugees and Returnees Service to expand access to work permits for forcibly displaced entrepreneurs. As preparations advance for the 2026 Africa Private Sector Forum on Displacement, the priority is to scale partnerships that connect policy reform, finance, enterprise support, and market access so more entrepreneurs can grow, create jobs, and participate fully in local and regional markets.

UNHCR

From a Pilot to a Growing Model: Livelihoods and Career Fair Reaches Benue State

Photo credit line: © UNHCR/Africa Communication Media Group

In June 2026, UNHCR, Jobberman Nigeria and the Benue State Government held the 2026 Livelihoods and Career Fair in Benue State, part of activities marking World Refugee Day. Building on a pilot held in Lagos in 2025, the Fair connected 12 private sector employers with more than 450 forcibly displaced persons, including entrepreneurs who exhibited their businesses and recorded sales nearly four times their average. By the close of the Fair, 18 displaced persons had been shortlisted for potential job placements, a number expected to grow through follow-up engagement.

Of the 450 displaced persons who took part, entrepreneurs and jobseekers engaged directly with First City Monument Bank (FCMB), one of 12 participating employers at the Fair. On the day, FCMB opened 28 new bank accounts for displaced persons, identified 11 entrepreneurs meeting requirements for business financing, and provided 25 point-of-sale terminals free of charge, giving entrepreneurs a way to accept payments and build a financial record. FCMB continues to engage eligible entrepreneurs on financing and remains open to supporting more refugee entrepreneurs who meet the requirements.

Together with the wider group of participating employers, the Fair connects displaced people to jobs, entrepreneurship support and financial services, building on the model piloted in Lagos and pointing toward its expansion to other states.
