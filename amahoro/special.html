If You Haven't Heard From Us, Here's What We've Been Building

If our updates haven't been reaching your inbox, you have missed quite a year and there is plenty to catch up on.

We launched Hiding in Plain Sight: Africa’s $27B Displacement Market Opportunity, a new publication highlighting investment opportunities across agriculture, finance, manufacturing, entrepreneurship and supply chains. In partnership with dfcu Bank, we are already translating its findings into practical financial products and pathways for displaced entrepreneurs across Uganda, drawing on the Bank’s 57-branch network and strong agribusiness and SME portfolio.

In Nigeria, a partnership with the Bank of Agriculture has already enabled approximately 10,000 displaced farmers to access financing, the first initiative of its kind in the Bank's 54-year history.

We have brought business leaders, investors and policymakers together from Kigali and Nairobi to Cape Town and Kampala to rethink how Africa invests in displaced talent.

We have also championed filmmakers at Cannes, opened pathways into the creative economy, connected employers to work-ready talent and spotlighted young innovators transforming their communities.

Through Agri Wednesdays, we have taken readers behind the scenes of the partnerships creating jobs, strengthening food systems and opening markets across the continent.

And as momentum builds towards the Africa Private Sector Forum on Displacement in Addis Ababa, the story is only getting bigger. Our newsletter brings you closer to the people, ideas and partnerships proving that displacement is not only a challenge to solve, it is talent, enterprise and opportunity waiting to be unlocked. How Ethiopia Inspired Amina’s Pan-African Story

For Lilly Bekele, Amina is more than a fashion brand. It is a tribute to heritage, memory and the generations that came before her. At its heart is Gabi, Ethiopia's centuries-old hand-spun, handwoven cotton, reimagined alongside textiles and craftsmanship from Burkina Faso, Mali, Côte d’Ivoire and across the continent.

The brand takes its name from Lilly's great-grandmother, Amina, who was forcibly displaced from Somalia to Ethiopia before settling in Harar. Her story of resilience, belonging and rebuilding continues to shape Lilly's vision for an Ethiopian-born brand with a Pan-African heart.

That vision has also translated into action. A partnership with Amahoro Coalition has enabled Amina to support the Coalition's internship programme and connect with displaced tailoring talent, ultimately leading to the hiring of two new team members. In doing so, Amina is putting its values of inclusion, dignity and opportunity into practice as the business grows.

In this conversation with Lilly we talk about the heritage behind Amina, her journey from development professional to entrepreneur, and why building a truly Pan-African business means creating opportunity along the way.

60 Days to Addis: The Next Act Begins

The countdown is officially on. In just 60 days, the Africa Private Sector Forum on Displacement will convene in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, from 15–18 November 2026 for its fourth edition.

Since Kigali in 2021, Accra in 2023 and Nairobi in 2025, the Forum has brought together more than 3,000 leaders across business, government, investment, development and displacement-affected communities. Along the way, one thing has become clear: economic inclusion works. The challenge now is taking what works to scale.

Under the theme, “The Next Act: Creating A More Inclusive Economic Ecosystem,” Addis 2026 will focus on scaling proven approaches to jobs, investment, enterprise growth, policy reform and market access for displaced people and host communities.

Join us in Addis Ababa as we move from what works to what can scale. Register your interest today to be part of the Africa Private Sector Forum on Displacement 2026.
