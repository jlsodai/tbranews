From a Historic First to a Growing Ecosystem in Nigeria



On the sidelines of the 2025 Africa Food Systems Forum in Dakar, Amahoro Coalition signed an agreement with Nigeria’s Bank of Agriculture to expand agricultural finance for refugees and internally displaced persons. The partnership established a dedicated Refugees and Displaced Persons Desk and helped embed inclusion across the Bank’s agricultural-financing portfolio. By the second quarter of 2026, the Bank had financed 8,500 displaced farmers; today, that figure stands at approximately 10,000, the first such financing in the Bank’s 54-year history. Its innovative model replaces traditional collateral with aggregator partnerships and in-kind, value-chain-secured credit.

What began as one partnership has since grown into a network of more than 20 aggregators, financiers, corporate off-takers and public institutions. Our own Roots2Market pilot reached 1,400 displaced farmers with credit, training, extension services and guaranteed markets, with more than 60% women’s participation. AgroLog has supported approximately 1,000 displaced farmers with inputs and climate-smart training, while SAO Group has brought 111 internally displaced people into paid employment. Together, these partnerships connect displaced people to finance, production, jobs and markets, creating pathways from short-term assistance to lasting economic independence. In this video, the Bank of Agriculture CEO, Ayo Sotinrin, explains how the partnership is transforming access to finance and sustainable livelihoods for displaced farmers, particularly women.

Closing Africa’s Catalytic Capital Gap



Amahoro Coalition convened a Catalytic Capital breakfast meeting, bringing together senior leaders and practitioners to explore how innovative finance can move from isolated pilots to systems-level impact. The discussion called for financing that better matches enterprises’ needs, more patient capital, stronger technical assistance and greater use of trusted local financial systems. Amahoro Coalition committed to building a pipeline of displaced entrepreneurs ready to absorb capital across Nigeria, Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda and South Africa.

The breakfast took place on the sidelines of the Africa Impact Summit 2026, held on 10–11 June at Ciela Resort in Lusaka, Zambia. Hosted by NABII Zambia and the Africa Impact Investing Group under the theme, “Beyond Borders: Scaling Impact and Innovation for Africa’s Sustainable Transformation,” the Summit brought together investors, policymakers and innovators working to advance sustainable development across the continent.

FELLOW SPOTLIGHT: How Davis Lyangenda is redesigning access to finance in frontier markets through Avigo Finance in Zambia


Born in Zambia’s Meheba Refugee Settlement to Angolan parents, David Lyangenda founded Avigo Finance to serve refugees and informal entrepreneurs excluded by conventional collateral requirements. Avigo uses village banks in which small groups collectively guarantee loans, helping repayment rates stabilises above 85%. Today, it serves 285 borrowers through 89 village banks—60% women and 75% from rural displaced and host communities. Through the Amahoro Fellowship, David is strengthening Avigo’s systems, pursuing formal registration and preparing to expand across Zambia, demonstrating that trust and community knowledge can be more effective measures of creditworthiness than paperwork.

What Morocco’s World Cup Run Teaches Us About Talent



Nearly two weeks have passed since the final whistle of the 2026 FIFA World Cup. The excitement may be beginning to fade, but Morocco’s performance still deserves attention. Reaching the quarter-finals for a second consecutive tournament confirmed that their historic 2022 run was no one-off, it was the return on nearly two decades of deliberate investment in grassroots scouting, elite academies and world-class infrastructure. It was also an undeniable proof that when talent is supported by the right systems, strong results follow.

For us at Amahoro Coalition, the lesson is clear: talent also exists in displaced communities, but the pathways to discover and develop it remain limited. In 2025, Amahoro and Samaritan Group brought together more than 120 refugee and host-community players for Ghana’s first Refugee Football Scouting Tournament, with 17 earning structured training opportunities and professional club trials. Looking ahead, the goal is to connect more displaced players to scouts, academies, clubs and private-sector partners, turning football into a pathway to livelihoods, belonging and long-term economic inclusion.
