International Youth Day

How innovations from displaced youth are transforming Africa


Observed annually on 12 August since 2000, International Youth Day raises awareness of the challenges and opportunities facing young people worldwide and celebrates their essential role in advancing sustainable development.

To mark the occasion, we showcased how innovations from young people with lived experience of displacement are transforming their communities across Africa. From Esther Kitumaini, who manufactures nutritious food products in Uganda, to Samson Younis Ousman, who provides digital financial services to refugee communities in Kenya and South Sudan, their stories demonstrate the potential of displaced youth as entrepreneurs, innovators and partners in development. They also highlight the barriers that continue to limit young people's access to education, employment and civic participation.

These stories show what becomes possible when those closest to a challenge are trusted and equipped to address it. The scale of this potential is significant: our latest report Hiding in Plain Sight: Africa's $27B Displacement Market Opportunity identifies more than 3.4 million refugee-led micro, small and medium-sized enterprises across Africa, with an estimated annual earning potential of $4 billion.

Private Sector Engagement

The Opportunity in Displacement: Advancing Economic Inclusion in South Africa

In Cape Town, Amahoro Coalition and UNHCR Partnerships Africa convened The Opportunity in Displacement, a high-level roundtable bringing together private sector leaders and other stakeholders to advance the economic inclusion of forcibly displaced people and host communities in South Africa.

The dialogue explored how government, business and other actors can coordinate more effectively to uphold refugees' right to work, and how investment in displacement-affected contexts can be de-risked to unlock greater economic opportunity.

Across South Africa, displaced people are already building businesses, contributing valuable skills to the workforce and strengthening local economies. Expanding access to jobs, markets and investment would enable more displaced people to participate fully in economic growth. This requires moving beyond viewing displacement solely through a humanitarian lens and recognising its place within Africa's value chains, markets and wider business ecosystem.

Refugee inclusion is both a humanitarian and economic imperative, with the potential to create shared value for businesses, displaced people and host communities. Amahoro Coalition and its partners will continue building an ecosystem in which the private sector helps lead this transformation.

The conversation continues at the Africa Private Sector Forum on Displacement, taking place in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia from the 16 to 18 November.

Agriculture & Finance

Turning Inclusion into Investment at the Africa Food Systems Forum

One year ago in Dakar, on the sidelines of the Africa Food Systems Forum, Amahoro Coalition and the Bank of Agriculture Nigeria signed a partnership aimed at creating up to 200,000 jobs for forcibly displaced people and host communities across Nigeria. The agreement established a framework for expanding access to finance and training while supporting agribusiness growth and rural development.

The partnership was built on a simple but important proposition: investing in agriculture within displaced communities is not charity. It is an economic strategy that can unlock new markets, strengthen food security and create dignified jobs. It also recognises that finance must go beyond credit by connecting people to skills, markets and the wider support needed to build sustainable livelihoods.

In this conversation, Amahoro Coalition Curator Isaac Kwaku Fokuo and Bank of Agriculture Managing Director and CEO Ayo Sotinrin reflect on how their organisations' complementary strengths can turn this ambition into action. By bringing together BOA's financial expertise and nationwide reach with Amahoro Coalition's community networks and understanding of displacement, the partnership offers a model for moving inclusive agriculture from isolated interventions to scale.

As the Africa Food Systems Forum marks its 20th edition in Kigali under the theme Investing in Africa's Agri-Food Systems: Nourishing Nations, Growing Jobs, Building Resilience, the message remains timely: displaced people must be recognised as farmers, entrepreneurs, workers and partners in building Africa's food security and rural prosperity.
